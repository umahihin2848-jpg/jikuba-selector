# M3 v2 Closing — Ticket Backtest (Wide / Quinella / Trio)

Date frozen: 2026-10-22
Status: research / shadow only. Production ticket logic is unchanged.

## Question
Does replacing only the M3 ranking from production v1 with the Closing-v2 shadow ranking improve the existing ticket policies for Wide, Quinella and Trio?

## Fixed ticket policies
Everything except the M3 rank was held fixed.

- Structural horses = M3 top 3 AND Ability top 3.
- Exact guard: exactly 2 structural horses; otherwise all three ticket routes STOP.
- Wide: structural pair, requires Ability #1 in the pair and field <=15. One ¥100 ticket.
- Quinella: Ability #1 structural horse + best Ability-ranked horse outside the structural pair. Both must be final win-popularity ranks 1–7. One ¥100 ticket.
- Trio: Pair Survival PASS only: exact structural pair + Ability #1 + field <=15 + pair contains M3 #1. Third row = final win-popularity top 4 excluding the pair. Four ¥100 tickets.

Important market-timing caveat: the historical dataset contains final popularity / final payouts. Wide selection is not popularity-dependent. Quinella eligibility and Trio third-row selection therefore represent a final-market retrospective backtest, not a strict 15-minute-before-post replay.

## Evaluation window
Primary comparison: 2025 + 2026 held-out-style period in the current research protocol. 2024 remains a model-selection/development year and is not counted as confirmatory ticket evidence.

## Statistical uncertainty
To allow same-day races to co-move, uncertainty was estimated with a cluster bootstrap by race date over the 2025–2026 evaluation dates. The estimand is the policy-level ROI difference v2 - v1.

- Wide ROI difference: +20.98 points; 95% cluster-bootstrap interval approximately -16.43 to +56.80 points.
- Quinella ROI difference: -12.60 points; interval approximately -57.54 to +32.89 points.
- Trio ROI difference: +18.43 points; interval approximately -38.75 to +76.30 points.

Hit-rate difference intervals were also wide:
- Wide: +6.75 points; approximately -1.44 to +15.06 points.
- Quinella: -2.52 points; approximately -6.74 to +1.12 points.
- Trio: +2.45 points; approximately -7.09 to +11.86 points.

None of the three ticket-level differences excludes zero at 95%. Three ticket types are being examined simultaneously, so a production promotion should be more conservative, not less.

## Stability / risk diagnostics
Maximum losing streak in the historical sequence:
- Wide: v1 18 -> v2 10.
- Quinella: v1 28 -> v2 29.
- Trio: v1 14 -> v2 11.

Maximum cumulative drawdown at ¥100 per combination:
- Wide: v1 -¥2,570 -> v2 -¥1,360.
- Quinella: v1 -¥4,880 -> v2 -¥7,270.
- Trio: v1 -¥5,600 -> v2 -¥8,240.

Trio is payout-sensitive: under v1 the largest single winning race supplied about 42.6% of total Trio return; under v2 about 32.6%. This remains a high-variance sample.

## Decision
### Wide
Promising shadow candidate. v2 improves both hit rate and ROI in both 2025 and 2026, and reduces the observed losing streak/drawdown. However, the date-clustered 95% intervals still cross zero. Do not replace production yet; record v1 and v2 Wide decisions prospectively.

### Quinella
Keep v1. v2 has worse combined hit rate/ROI and materially worse 2026 performance. No production change.

### Trio
Keep v1 in production; v2 stays shadow. The combined point estimate improves, but 2025 is essentially flat and the 2026 gain is too small-sample / payout-sensitive to justify a rule change.

## Production rule
No production ticket logic change from this backtest alone.
- Wide: v2 shadow comparison ON.
- Quinella: production v1 unchanged; no v2 promotion.
- Trio: v2 shadow comparison ON, production v1 unchanged.
- Pair Survival remains based on production M3 v1 until separately validated.
